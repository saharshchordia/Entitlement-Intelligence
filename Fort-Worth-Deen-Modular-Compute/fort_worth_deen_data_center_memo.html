Data centers

Use listed in I / J / K

Current path: the industrial use table permits data centers in I, J, and K; ordinary development approvals still apply. The adopted August 11 application rule requires grid-approval documentation for covered City filings. The City also says its updated water policy governs new data centers in its water service area.

Policy gate: Council initiated a moratorium process but had not adopted a 90-day pause as of this review. The July draft data-center zoning standards were not enacted; the Zoning Commission recommended denial 7-4. A later adopted moratorium or code amendment would change this lane.

Industrial outdoor storage

Classification dependent

Current path: K allows the table's outdoor-storage and terminal uses, but stand-alone/non-accessory outside storage requires a Council CUP limited to five years. Where actual activity is a truck terminal, section 5.139 supplies traffic and separation conditions.

Limit: parking trucks or trailers is not automatically the same as a terminal, and a permitted storage row does not waive the non-accessory CUP rule. The site's primary operation and relationship between storage, parking, and charging control the classification.

Scope
District-specific use table; K example here
Authority
City zoning review; Zoning Commission advisory; Council for CUP
Evidence
Use table, storage, terminal

EV charging

Principal use unresolved

Current path: chargers accessory to a lawful site activity are reviewed with that activity and applicable electrical/site permits. Fort Worth's published table does not name stand-alone EV charging as a principal use, so this record does not establish a by-right charging-yard path even in K.

Limit: a truck-charging campus with dwell time, staging, storage, or retail service may implicate different use rows. The 2025 Meacham action conveyed an Oncor easement for airport-lot chargers; it was not a zoning determination for an independent truck-charging yard.

Scope
Citywide classification; parcel zoning controls the associated use
Authority

Evidence
Use table, electrical permit, Meacham action

Operative Rules And Approval Sequence

Data-center definition
Code section 9.101 defines a data center by buildings/structures designed or modified for networked computers, data processing, and support equipment, serving third parties or a single user. It states no MW, water-cooling, or minimum-size threshold. The City's FAQ offers a shorter description; the codified definition governs the zoning read.
Data-center use and gate
Section 4.803 lists data centers P in I, J, and K. Council's August 11 application requirement is in force for covered local development filings; staff described PUCT/ERCOT proof for pre-development conferences, plats/replats, building permits, and infrastructure plan review, with annexation requests excluded. Council transcript; City status. This does not prove actual grid capacity at Deen Road.
Moratorium and drafts
The City's current status page says Council initiated, but had not yet enacted, the legal process for a 90-day data-center moratorium. First hearing is scheduled by November 10, 2026; tentative adoption is February 16, 2027, subject to hearings and a Council supermajority. June/July zoning and noise controls are proposed only.
Water and operating impacts
The City status page labels the August 11 water-policy update adopted for new data centers in the Fort Worth Water Utility service area, including closed-loop cooling and NSIU wastewater permitting. The same page calls for water loading and wastewater studies as applicable. Industrial districts currently lack a data-center-specific maximum sound level; the proposed ambient-plus-5-dBA standard is not current law.
IOS use and CUP
Section 4.803 lists outdoor storage P* in I/J/K; section 5.133 requires a Council CUP for non-accessory outside storage, limited to five years, with security, six-foot opaque screening, and drainage conditions. A truck terminal in K is listed P, subject to section 5.139 traffic-plan, private maneuvering, and 200-foot residential separation rules. Operational classification determines the lane.
EV charging
The reviewed use table does not name EV charging as a principal use. Charger installation at an otherwise lawful activity is distinct from permission for a dedicated charging/truck-staging yard. The City's electrical permit process addresses equipment, not principal-use classification; applicable site, parking, accessibility, fire, utility, and electrical reviews remain.

No customer, power amount, charger count, truck count, or cooling method is assumed. ERCOT/PUCT and utility review concern electrical service; they do not replace the City's land-use classification. The City's grid-documentation gate does not itself confer a connection approval.
