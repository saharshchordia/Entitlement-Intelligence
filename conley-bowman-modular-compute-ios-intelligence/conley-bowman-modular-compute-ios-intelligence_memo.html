Jurisdiction Summary

  • County parcel GIS identifies an unincorporated 5.52-acre M (light industrial) parcel with LIND future land use and BVT4 overlay. Tier 4 is the industrial subarea of the Bouldercrest-Cedar Grove-Moreland Overlay District, intended for industrial redevelopment under higher design standards. It is not a separate base zoning district or a project approval: its use table controls where it conflicts with M zoning, and uses not listed in that table are not allowed.
  • The county's temporary moratorium defines a data center by computing/service/data infrastructure, not by a MW threshold. If modular compute meets that definition, applications and permits for new or expanded facilities are paused through September 30, 2026 under the last verified Board extension.
  • The Board denied proposed permanent data-center definitions and size categories on June 23, 2026. Those proposed categories are not operative law. The Tier 4 table does not name data centers, so modular compute's permanent use classification remains unresolved.
  • For IOS/EV trucking, the Tier 4 table distinguishes a storage yard (SLUP) from a truck stop or terminal (YES) and transportation-equipment storage (YES). Which row fits the actual program is unresolved. The 2024 EV-charging amendment does not by itself establish a principal truck-yard entitlement.
  • A combined use needs independent classification of both activities. County meeting records show concern about data-center energy, water, noise, and neighborhood compatibility, but do not establish a site-specific approval or disapproval for this parcel.

Data Center Definition

The adopted moratorium resolution covers a physical room, building, or facility housing infrastructure for building, running, delivering, or transmitting applications and services, or storing/managing associated data. It states no numeric electrical-load threshold. Whether a particular modular-compute installation meets each element depends on its actual function. This definition governs the temporary moratorium; it is not a new permanent Tier 4 permitted-use row.

Planning staff proposed accessory, minor, medium, major, and campus categories with size and substation distinctions. The Board denied that text amendment on June 23, so none of those proposed thresholds is an operative classification rule. Under overlay section 3.39.3, an unlisted use is not allowed and the director determines whether a proposed activity fits a listed row, except where a SLUP is required.

Moratorium And Application Gate

The December resolution bars acceptance and grant/denial of new or expanded data-center SLUP, rezoning, land-disturbance, building, business-license, and occupancy applications in unincorporated DeKalb. It excepts lawful existing operations. The June 9 Board summary records a 100-day extension from June 23 through September 30, 2026. A July extension item is marked preliminary without a final action in the inspected docket; it cannot establish a later deadline. This memo is current as of September 22, 2026 and expiration remains a time-sensitive check.

Local Rules And Approval Requirements

The Tier 4 overlay supersedes conflicting underlying M rules. Its table lists storage yard as T4-SP, truck stop or terminal and transportation-equipment storage/maintenance as YES, and utility categories separately. The overlay also prohibits outdoor operations from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. likely to create noise or odor disturbing nearby occupants. A standalone substation or transmission structure has its own table classification; it is not automatically an accessory to either scenario. Division 39, sections 3.39.2-3 and use table.

DeKalb adopted an EV-charging substitute in August 2024. The county packet addresses chargers as accessory structures with site-design controls, while the final Board minutes establish adoption of a substitute. The precise final codified dimensional language should be checked before applying a setback or height to a plan. For construction, county permitting guidance describes plan review, land development, building, and inspection processes. None of these process rules is proof that the principal use is allowed.
