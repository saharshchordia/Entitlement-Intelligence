# Jurupa Valley Cross-Case Transcript Findings

Machine transcripts were generated for the Council video target set. Use raw transcripts and source videos for verification before direct quotation.

## Main Finding

Jurupa Valley does not treat IOS, truck storage, pallet yards, or other truck-intensive outdoor industrial uses as casual light-industrial optionality. The transcript-backed pattern is stricter: M-H plus CUP is the narrow gateway, and even that gateway can fail where findings, safety, compatibility, code history, or residential proximity are weak.

## Repeated Decision Factors

- Truck traffic, neighborhood compatibility, pedestrian safety, air/noise impacts, and infrastructure wear drove the 2021 policy regime.
- M-SC, M-M, and I-P are weak bases for IOS/truck-intensive underwriting after the 2021 ordinance.
- M-H eligibility is necessary but not sufficient. FC Pallets shows M-H rezoning/CUP/variance/SDP can still fail.
- Applicant credibility matters. Long-running noncompliance or informal expansion hurts the case.
- Large approvals require large packages: EIR/MND, findings, MMRP, development agreement, public benefits, site/civil design, frontage/offsite work, and infrastructure commitments.
- Consent-calendar items can confirm procedural outcomes, but they often do not provide decision-making texture.

## Capital Readiness Pattern

The recurring capital buckets are legislative entitlement work, CEQA, traffic/freight analysis, road/frontage improvements, fire access and water supply, site hardening, fencing/screening, landscaping, stormwater/drainage, operating controls, and cleanup/removal of unpermitted activity.

## EV Charging Contrast

EV charging should be framed as a cleaner infrastructure use compared with IOS/truck storage. The key is to avoid looking like de facto truck storage: no uncontrolled overnight parking, no material storage, no diesel idling pattern, no ROW spillover, and a strong utility/fire/circulation plan.
